Hi! My name is Naznien Fevrianne Malano, but people usually call me Naznien. Some shorten it to Naz or Nien. My childhood friends usually call me Naz, while these days most people call me Nien. Both are totally fine with me.

I work at GoTo Group as an Associate Data Scientist in the AI/LLM Group. My work mainly involves integrating AI/LLMs, knowledge bases, and backend systems into agentic workflows that can actually solve users’ problems.

Outside of work, I actually do quite a lot of other things. :D

When I was younger, I competed in archery quite seriously and won several regional championships. I stopped before high school, simply because my parents wanted me to focus on getting into a good high school and, eventually, a good university. Yeah, you know, the usual Asian parent trajectory. :D

High school was also when I went through a bit of an existential crisis.

I did eventually get into one of the top high schools I had been aiming for, after studying hard and going to all sorts of tutoring classes (thanks to my parents for making all of that possible). But I was never a straight-A student, nor was I one of those students who consistently ranked at the top of the class. If anything, I remember being surrounded by people who seemed incredibly smart and feeling like I was constantly trying to catch up.

I also wasn’t used to studying that hard. Before high school, academics had never really been the center of my life. For the longest time, what I actually wanted was to become a competitive archer and eventually make it to Indonesia’s national training team. So at the time, I was still learning to accept that I had to choose between seriously pursuing archery and focusing on school, and that, ultimately, I had chosen school.

I think that left me a little lost for a while. I had spent so much of my younger years knowing exactly what I wanted to be, and suddenly I didn’t anymore.

But high school was also when I started discovering so many things outside of academics that genuinely fascinated me. I got really into films, started reading more, became increasingly interested in politics and international affairs, and slowly realized that there were so many other things in the world I could care deeply about.

So even though I took the science track in high school, all of my friends were convinced that I would eventually study something in the humanities or arts. And honestly, that made sense. I was really into politics, loved reading fiction in my spare time, and was crazy about films.

For a while, I really wanted to study International Relations, simply because I loved keeping up with international politics. I was also considering Philosophy, mostly because I had recently finished reading Sophie’s World (lol I know that sounds very shallow, but mind you, I was only 18 at the time haha)

I wanted International Relations badly enough that, despite being a science-track student, I considered taking the social sciences track for the UTBK. I even spent my school break trying to catch up on three years’ worth of history, geography, sociology, and other social science subjects, from Grade 10 all the way to Grade 12, before starting my final year of high school.

Long story short ... I somehow ended up getting accepted into one of the most established computer science programs in Indonesia, the Faculty of Computer Science at the University of Indonesia. :D

Why? Because my parents told me to give the science track a try. The problem was, I didn’t like physics or biology. Out of all the science subjects, math was the most tolerable one for me. So I thought, why not pick a major that mostly involves math? And that’s how I ended up choosing Computer Science. :D

And the funny thing is, I had never really coded before.

I wrote my first-ever “Hello, World!” in college. During that time, I also taught archery to younger kids in my spare time to earn some extra money. One of the perks was that I got to practice archery for free after my students’ training sessions. :D

Somewhere along the way, I fell in love with statistics and probability, linear algebra, and AI and data science. A huge part of that was because I was lucky enough to have really great lecturers at university. I genuinely enjoyed those classes and would usually score somewhere between 85 and 100 on the final exams. :D

It was kind of funny considering how I felt about academics in high school. The person who once felt like she was constantly trying to catch up suddenly found subjects she genuinely enjoyed studying. I also ended up becoming teaching assistant in every subjects that related to AI/data science.

That interest eventually led me to an internship at GoTo Group, which turned into a full-time role exactly five days after my thesis defense. 

University, especially the environment and the lecturers I met there, also made me realize that I genuinely love learning. I became so used to studying that even now, while working full-time, I still find myself wanting to take courses, pick up new skills, and learn something completely new.

These days, some of that learning has nothing to do with my job. I spend some of my weekends taking piano and tennis lessons.

I’m also kind of obsessed with music. I taught myself how to play guitar when I was a kid, mostly because of John Mayer. My music taste has expanded a lot since then. I listen to Fiona Apple, Jeff Buckley, Hozier, Fairuz, and so many others. Honestly, I listen to way too much music to list everything here. I have a ridiculous number of [Spotify playlists](https://open.spotify.com/user/nfmalano?si=HYQKy2sXRcOcwUyLgC_Cgg&utm_source=copy-link) that probably explain my taste better than I ever could, haha.

And then there are books. I love reading, especially works by writers like Elif Shafak and Arundhati Roy. One of my reading goals is to read more books by women in translation and discover literature from different parts of the world. I share what I’m currently reading, and occasionally my thoughts about the books, on [@shereadsclub on Instagram](https://www.instagram.com/shereadsclub) and on [my Goodreads](https://www.goodreads.com/user/show/25631335)

I also love watching tennis, especially the Grand Slams. My favorite players are Carlos Alcaraz and Iga Świątek.

I love Alcaraz because of his almost childlike enthusiasm and how exciting his game is to watch. I sometimes struggle with watching best-of-five matches in men’s tennis because they can feel really long and, honestly, a little boring at times. But somehow, I never find myself bored watching Alcaraz play. There’s always something interesting happening on court.

I love Iga Świątek for different reasons. I really admire her determination, agility, and attitude on court. I’m still rooting for her to win more Grand Slams and eventually make her way back to world No. 1. :D

So, yeah. In short, I work with AI for a living, used to shoot arrows competitively, wasn’t much of an academic growing up, but somehow ended up falling in love with linear algebra. In my spare time, I love watching tennis and hyperfixating over some books/films/musics. 😁
